## About Us

The **Perception, Sensing, and Instrumentation (PSI) Lab** at **Texas A&M University**, directed by [Prof. Ricardo Gutierrez-Osuna](https://engineering.tamu.edu/cse/profiles/rgutierrez-osuna.html), develops machine learning and signal processing methods for human-centered technologies.

Our work spans **speech processing** and **digital health**: from real-time, privacy-preserving speech systems to wearable sensing and data-driven approaches for understanding physiological signals. Across both areas, we study robust representations, efficient models, and intelligent systems that work beyond controlled laboratory settings.

## Research Areas

### Speech & Spoken Communication

We build models that analyze, transform, and generate speech while preserving the information that matters for communication.

- **Voice conversion and speech synthesis:** Streaming and low-latency methods for controllable speech generation.
- **Speaker privacy:** Anonymization techniques designed to protect speaker identity while preserving linguistic content.
- **Accent and pronunciation modeling:** Accent classification, adaptation, and approaches supporting non-native speech and pronunciation learning.
- **Robust speech representations:** Methods for disentangling speaker, linguistic, and prosodic information.

### Digital Health & Physiological Sensing

We develop data resources and machine learning methods that use physiological signals to investigate health-related questions.

- **Wearable biosignal analysis:** Learning from ECG, PPG, electrodermal activity, accelerometry, and other sensor streams.
- **Glucose monitoring and diabetes research:** Exploring non-invasive estimation, hypoglycemia prediction, and multimodal physiological modeling.
- **Personalized nutrition:** Understanding glucose responses to meals and other lifestyle factors.
- **Reliable evaluation:** Data processing and assessment tools for physiological prediction models.

Our broader research also includes **active sensing, chemical sensing, biofeedback, and assistive technologies**.
